Medical Terminology and Abbreviations

Medical Terminology and Abbreviations

Oct 26, 20268 min read

Overview

Medical terminology provides a standardized way to communicate clinical information.

Many terms are formed using Greek or Latin elements:

Prefix + Root + Suffix

  • Prefix: Modifies meaning, often indicating location, number, or intensity.
  • Root: Identifies the main structure or concept.
  • Suffix: Indicates a condition, process, or specialty.

Examples

TermComponentsMeaning
HyperthyroidismHyper- + thyroid + -ismCondition of excessive thyroid activity
CardiologyCardi/o + -logyStudy of the heart
PolyneuropathyPoly- + neur/o + -pathyDisease affecting multiple peripheral nerves

Abbreviation Safety

The Joint Commission identifies several abbreviations and medication notations that create significant error risks.

Examples to Avoid in Orders and Medication-Related Documentation

AvoidWrite Instead
U or uunit
IUinternational unit
QD, q.d.daily
QOD, q.o.d.every other day
MSWrite the complete intended medication name
MSO4 / MgSO4Write the complete intended medication name
Trailing zero, e.g. 5.0 mg5 mg
Missing leading zero, e.g. .5 mg0.5 mg

These restrictions apply to the contexts specified by the relevant standards and organizational policies. They are not a blanket prohibition against every medical abbreviation.

CNA Responsibilities

  • Learn abbreviations used by your facility.
  • Use only accepted terminology in documentation.
  • Ask for clarification when an abbreviation is unclear.
  • Avoid ambiguous shortcuts in verbal reports.
  • Do not guess when instructions may affect resident safety.
