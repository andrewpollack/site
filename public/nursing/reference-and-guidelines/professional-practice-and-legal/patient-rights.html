Patient Rights

Patient Rights

Oct 26, 20266 min read

Quick Reference

Residents have the right to receive safe, respectful, individualized care that preserves their dignity, autonomy, privacy, independence, and quality of life.
These rights apply regardless of a resident’s diagnosis, abilities, or payment source.

Core Resident Rights

Dignity & Respect

  • Receive respectful, individualized care.
  • Be free from discrimination, humiliation, and punishment.
  • Maintain personal identity, preferences, and relationships.
  • Receive reasonable accommodations for individual needs.

Autonomy & Independence

  • Participate in developing and revising the care plan.
  • Make informed choices about care and daily routines.
  • Consent to or refuse treatment, subject to applicable law.
  • Maintain independence in activities of daily living when possible.
  • Choose personal clothing, grooming, and activities.

Privacy & Confidentiality

  • Receive personal care with appropriate privacy.
  • Have personal and medical information kept confidential.
  • Maintain privacy in personal communications.
  • Keep personal belongings protected.
  • Access personal and medical records under applicable rules.

Personal Property & Finances

  • Retain and use personal belongings as permitted by safety and space requirements.
  • Manage personal finances.
  • Choose whether to authorize the facility to manage personal funds.
  • Receive protection against misuse or theft of personal property.

Safety & Freedom from Mistreatment

  • Receive care in a safe, clean, and comfortable environment.
  • Be free from abuse, neglect, and exploitation.
  • Be free from improper physical or chemical restraints.
  • Be free from involuntary seclusion.

Communication & Complaints

  • Communicate with family, friends, advocates, and authorized representatives.
  • Access the long-term care ombudsman.
  • Raise grievances without retaliation.
  • Receive information about rights and available complaint procedures.

End-of-Life Decisions

  • Participate in decisions about treatment goals.
  • Exercise applicable rights concerning advance directives.
  • Receive care consistent with authorized treatment preferences and medical orders.

CNA Responsibilities

Respect Choices:

  • Explain care before beginning.
  • Offer appropriate choices.
  • Encourage independence.
  • Respect preferences, privacy, and dignity.
    Protect Confidentiality:
  • Discuss resident information only with authorized individuals for legitimate care-related purposes.
  • Protect records and personal belongings.
  • Never photograph or share resident information on personal devices or social media without proper authorization.
    Respond to Refusals:
  • Do not force routine care.
  • Clarify concerns and offer appropriate alternatives.
  • Notify the nurse when care is refused or cannot be completed.
    Recognize & Report Violations:
  • Watch for possible abuse, neglect, exploitation, or rights violations.
  • Protect immediate resident safety.
  • Report suspected violations promptly through required channels.
  • Follow applicable mandated reporting requirements.

Transfer & Discharge Protections

Residents of federally regulated nursing facilities have protections against improper transfers and discharges.

  • Transfer or discharge must meet legally permitted grounds.
  • Written notice generally must be provided at least 30 days in advance, subject to specified exceptions.
  • Notices must include reasons, timing, destination, appeal information, and ombudsman contact details as applicable.
  • Residents must receive appropriate preparation for safe, orderly transfers or discharges.
  • Bed-hold and readmission rights depend on the applicable requirements.

All residents in long-term care have the right to stay at their chosen facility unless:

  • Facility cannot meet the needs of the resident;
  • the resident no longer needs SNF services,
  • the resident is threatening the safety or health of staff or otehr residents, or
  • the resident cannot pay for services.

Notice of transfer must include where resident is going, contact info for state ombudsman, and info on the bed-hold and readmission policies.
CNA Responsibility: Respect resident rights, maintain dignity, support safe preparation, and refer questions about legal notices or discharge decisions to the appropriate licensed staff.
Source: 42 CFR § 483.15 — Admission, Transfer, and Discharge Rights

Long-Term Care Ombudsman & Complaints

The Long-Term Care Ombudsman Program advocates for residents of long-term care facilities.

Ombudsmen may:

  • Explain resident rights.
  • Receive and help address resident complaints.
  • Advocate for residents’ wishes and interests.
  • Help residents understand available complaint procedures.
  • Support improvements in long-term care quality.

Ombudsman complaint handling generally respects resident direction, consent, and confidentiality requirements.

California Department of Public Health

The California Department of Public Health (CDPH) licenses and regulates certain healthcare facilities, including skilled nursing facilities.

Residents, family members, employees, or other concerned individuals may submit complaints to CDPH.

Important: An ombudsman complaint or internal grievance process does not replace a CNA’s independent mandatory-reporting duty when abuse-reporting laws apply.

CNA Responsibilities

  • Inform residents about available help according to facility procedures.
  • Respect their right to make complaints without retaliation.
  • Promptly report concerns through required channels.
  • Do not discourage or interfere with a complaint.
  • Do not promise secrecy when mandatory reporting is legally required.

Important Considerations

  • Resident rights continue even when a resident requires extensive assistance.
  • Cognitive impairment does not automatically eliminate decision-making rights.
  • A resident’s authorized representative may participate in certain decisions as permitted by law.
  • Protecting safety does not justify unnecessary restrictions on autonomy.
  • Facility convenience is not a valid reason to disregard resident rights.
  • CNAs support resident decision-making but do not independently determine legal capacity or obtain informed consent for procedures requiring licensed clinical responsibility.
