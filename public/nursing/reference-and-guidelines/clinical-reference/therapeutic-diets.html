Therapeutic Diets

Therapeutic Diets

Oct 26, 20264 min read

Quick Reference

Therapeutic diets are individualized food and fluid plans used to support medical and nutritional needs.

Gluten-Free Diet

For celiac disease, gluten-containing grains include:

  • Wheat.
  • Barley.
  • Rye.
  • Triticale.

Naturally gluten-free options include rice, corn, quinoa, potatoes, and other appropriate foods.

Check for cross-contact and hidden gluten in ingredients.

Postoperative Diets

Common diet orders include:

  • NPO: Nothing by mouth, except as explicitly authorized.
  • Clear liquids: Transparent liquids allowed by the prescribed plan.
  • Full liquids: A wider range of liquids and foods that become liquid, as defined by the order.
  • Soft diet: Foods requiring less chewing or otherwise modified as directed.
  • Regular diet: Foods without a prescribed texture restriction, subject to other diet orders.

Do not independently advance a resident’s diet.

Enteral & Parenteral Nutrition

Enteral Nutrition:

  • Delivers nutrition into the gastrointestinal tract using a feeding tube.
  • May use nasogastric, gastrostomy, or other approved access.
  • Can be continuous or intermittent.

Total Parenteral Nutrition (TPN):

  • Delivers prescribed nutrients intravenously.
  • Bypasses the gastrointestinal tract.
  • Requires specialized clinical management.

CNA Responsibilities:

  • Protect tubing and equipment during routine care.
  • Follow positioning and mobility restrictions.
  • Observe and report concerning symptoms or equipment problems.
  • Do not independently administer or adjust enteral or parenteral nutrition.

Fluid Restrictions

Residents may have individualized restrictions related to heart, kidney, or other medical conditions.

  • Follow the ordered daily fluid allowance.
  • Record intake as assigned.
  • Include foods counted as fluids under facility policy.
  • Respect the resident’s preferences and choices.
  • Report requests that conflict with the care plan or concerns about dehydration.
  • Never independently change a fluid restriction.

Diabetes & Meal Safety

  • Verify the prescribed meal and meal timing.
  • Follow ordered glucose-check and meal-coordination procedures.
  • Report poor intake or a missed meal promptly.
  • Observe for symptoms concerning for hypoglycemia or hyperglycemia.
  • Do not independently administer glucose-lowering treatment or change medication timing.

Digestive Problems & Nutrition

Nausea and Vomiting

  • Observe frequency, amount, and associated symptoms.
  • Follow prescribed diet and fluid restrictions.
  • Provide approved comfort and hygiene assistance.
  • Report persistent vomiting, abdominal pain, or inability to tolerate intake.

Diarrhea

  • Offer timely toileting assistance.
  • Follow infection-prevention precautions.
  • Provide appropriate skin cleansing and protection.
  • Observe for changes in stool and possible dehydration.
  • Report new, severe, or persistent diarrhea.

Malnutrition

Possible concerns include:

  • Persistent inadequate intake.
  • Unintentional weight loss.
  • Weakness or functional decline.
  • Poor wound healing.

Report observations rather than independently diagnosing malnutrition.

Important Considerations

  • Always verify that the resident receives the correct meal.
  • Diet restrictions, food textures, and fluid consistencies are resident-specific.
  • Never substitute foods or liquids when their safety or compatibility is uncertain.
  • Respect food preferences, culture, and informed refusal.
  • Do not independently modify diet orders or nutrition treatments.
